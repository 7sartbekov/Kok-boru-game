How a match goes

Ride over the loose ulak to pick it up. It weighs about 30 kg, so Sarmat slows down while you carry it and you can only heave it a short way.

Taking the ulak from another rider is a tug-of-war. When a Chüy rider comes alongside you, a grip bar appears under the two horses. Hold E to keep hold of it, or sprint to break away. When you ride alongside a Chüy carrier, tap E or Space fast (or click) to pull it from him. Each tap is a hard yank. Two riders pulling together are stronger, and teammates riding next to the carrier help him hold on.

Carry it close. Once you are inside the gold dashed ring around the Chüy kazan, the kazan lights up and says THROW, and your heave goes in. Ride right up to the kazan and Sarmat drops it in by himself. Away from the kazan, a throw is a short pass to a teammate.

Sprinting drains the gold stamina bar, and it refills while you ride normally.

On a phone, drag on the field to steer and use the round buttons. Turn the phone sideways for a bigger field.

Field notes

Kök börüкөк бөрү
Literally "grey wolf". The usual origin story is herders on horseback chasing down a wolf that attacked the flock and fighting over the carcass.
Ulakулак
The goat carcass both teams fight over, 30 to 32 kg under the federation rules. In this game it is drawn as a plain felt bundle.
Kazanказан
The raised goal at each end of the field. Riders can't ride through it, so the ulak has to be thrown in.
Chabandesчабандес
The rider. The game is won in shoulder-to-shoulder fights at a gallop, and by a horse that still has something left at the end.
The sideline
Each bench has two substitutes waiting on their horses, two managers and the coach, who paces and waves when his team gets close to the kazan. The referee rides alongside the play in yellow. Fans fill the wooden stands along the bottom and jump up with their flags when their side scores.

Rules in this game

Taken from the Kök Börü Federation of the Kyrgyz Republic rules (2024) and simplified so the game stays quick and easy.

Left out on purpose: offside in the penalty area, the bullit shoot-out, swapping kazans between periods, 12-rider squads and the two-minute capture limit.

Four a side
Each team has four riders on the field, as in the real game.
Opening ceremony
While the Kök Börü anthem plays, the teams line up on the field, ride around the tai-kazans, greet each other in the middle and line up in front of the stands. The people in the stands give the bata, the blessing, from their seats with their hands held together, and the riders join in on horseback. Everyone says "Oomiyin" and passes their hands over their face. Only then does the match begin.
Oath
Then the riders take the start line and swear to play fair.
Periods
Three periods of one minute each instead of fifteen. If the score is level at the end, the next salym wins.
Salymсалым
A goal: the ulak lands in the kazan. Play restarts from the start line with the ulak in the centre circle.
Jekeme-jekeжекеме-жеке
If the ulak goes out of the field, play stops. You and one Chüy rider face off in the nearest circle while everyone else waits outside it.
Snatching
Riders may push with their horses and bodies but may not grab the other rider, so the fight is over the carcass itself. Because it weighs about 30 kg, winning it takes a long pull, not a quick grab.
Rotation
Horses tire as they play and slow down. A tired rider rides to his team's bench at the top touchline and swaps with a fresh rider and horse waiting there, as in the real rule where the two must touch at the line. Sarmat stays in for the whole match.
Penalty box
Ramming at speed into a rider who is picking up or holding the ulak is a foul. The Chüy rider who does it sits out in the penalty box for 20 seconds, standing in for two minutes.
